## **Fabry-Perot Cavity Experiment for Precision Laser Spectroscopy**

This internship report documents the experimental and theoretical study of a Fabry-Perot cavity and its application in precision laser spectroscopy.  
The project was conducted under the supervision of **Dr. Arijit Sharma** at the **Department of Physics, IIT Tirupati** during **14th May to 14th July 2024**.

---

## **Overview**

The Fabry-Perot cavity is an optical device consisting of two parallel mirrors that create multiple beam interference by reflecting light back and forth.  
This experiment demonstrates how the Fabry-Perot cavity can be used to improve spectral resolution by producing sharp interference fringes, which can be analyzed for laser and optical applications.

---

## **Theoretical Background**

**Interference:**  
The principle of interference relies on the superposition of light waves, producing constructive or destructive interference depending on phase differences.

**Multiple Beam Interference:**  
Light beams reflected multiple times within the cavity interfere to produce narrow transmission peaks.

**Optical Cavities:**  
The Fabry-Perot cavity is a type of optical cavity used in high-resolution spectroscopy by trapping light and enhancing interference effects.

**Key Concepts Discussed Include:**
- Free Spectral Range (FSR)  
- Finesse of the cavity  
- Line Width and Full Width Half Maximum (FWHM)  
- Round-trip time and circulating intensity  
- Cavity transmission coefficient  
- Transverse Electromagnetic (TEM) modes  

---

## **Experimental Procedure**

**Beam Path Alignment:**  
Laser beam height measured and aligned using mirrors and beam splitters.

**Confocal Cavity Setup:**  
Lenses and mirrors arranged in a confocal configuration, where cavity length equals mirror radii, providing stable light propagation.

**Laser Alignment:**  
Laser injected into the Fabry-Perot cavity and tuned for resonance by adjusting cavity length.

**Detection:**  
Transmitted light detected by a photodetector and analyzed using an oscilloscope.

---

## **Data Analysis**

Data acquired from the photodetector was recorded on an oscilloscope and converted from time to frequency scale.  
Side bands and frequency spacing were identified corresponding to modulation frequencies.  
Lorentzian curve fitting was applied to the resonance peaks to determine peak parameters accurately.  
The Full Width Half Maximum (FWHM) of resonance peaks was calculated and compared with theoretical values.

---

## **Results and Observations**

- Experimental FWHM was found to be approximately **0.26 MHz**, close to the theoretical calculation of **0.33 MHz**.  
- The narrower experimental linewidth suggests higher finesse and lower loss in the cavity than initially estimated.  
- The experiment verifies that the Fabry-Perot cavity effectively traps light, allowing photons to circulate longer, enhancing spectral resolution.

---

## **Conclusion**

This project successfully demonstrated the precision capabilities of the **Fabry-Perot cavity** in laser spectroscopy.  
The experimental results closely matched theoretical predictions, validating the cavity design and measurement techniques.  
This work deepens understanding of **optical resonators** and their applications in **high-resolution spectral measurements**.
